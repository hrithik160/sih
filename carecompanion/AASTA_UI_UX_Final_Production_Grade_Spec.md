# AASTA — Non-AI-Looking UI/UX Design Specification

## 1. Core Visual Direction

AASTA must intentionally avoid the visual language of a typical AI-generated SaaS prototype.

The interface should feel human-designed, restrained, familiar, calm, and trustworthy rather than futuristic or synthetic.

### Avoid

- Excessive gradients
- Neon blue/purple AI aesthetics
- Glowing blobs
- Glassmorphism
- Excessive floating cards
- Excessive rounded containers
- Decorative AI circuitry
- Repeated "AI-powered" messaging
- Too many badges
- Excessive emoji
- Childish mascot-heavy interfaces
- Huge illustrations with little meaningful content
- Constant animated or pulsing elements
- Futuristic healthcare holograms
- Glowing AI brains
- Robot imagery
- Artificial-looking stock photography

### Prefer

- Strong typography
- Generous whitespace
- Real photography or restrained illustrations
- Warm neutral backgrounds
- One primary brand color
- One warm accent color
- Subtle borders
- Modest corner radius
- Limited shadows
- Strong alignment and grid discipline
- Clear information hierarchy
- Real product screenshots
- Deliberate content density
- Natural human imagery
- Familiar visual cues

### Desired Emotional Character

**Calm**  
Not clinical or cold.

**Human**  
Not synthetic or AI-first.

**Trustworthy**  
Not flashy.

**Familiar**  
Not futuristic.

**Accessible**  
Not minimal to the point of ambiguity.

---

## 2. Brand Visual Positioning

Do not make AI the visual or messaging hero.

Avoid making phrases such as:

> AI-Powered Cognitive Care Platform

the dominant visual identity.

AI-related capabilities should not visually dominate the product.

The product identity should be communicated through human-centered design, clarity, accessibility, and restraint.

---

## 3. Public-Facing Visual Design

The public-facing experience should feel like a polished, real healthcare product rather than a hackathon or AI demonstration.

### Visual Direction

Use believable human/family imagery:

- Older adults
- Family members or caregivers nearby
- Phone or tablet usage
- Natural environments
- Warm, natural lighting
- Indian context where appropriate

Avoid:

- Futuristic healthcare imagery
- Artificial-looking elderly stock photography
- Glowing AI brains
- Robot imagery
- Generic "AI technology" illustrations
- Decorative circuitry

Use actual product screenshots where possible instead of generic dashboard imagery.

---

## 4. Visual Design System

### Color

Suggested direction:

- Primary: Deep teal / blue-green
- Accent: Warm amber/orange
- Background: Warm off-white
- Text: Near-black / dark charcoal
- Success: Green
- Warning: Amber
- Emergency: Red

Use color together with text and/or icons. Never rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
If you believe any other colors are good, apply it but dont use the colors which make it obvious AI generated.

### Typography

Patient-facing interfaces should prioritize readability:

- Page title: 28–32sp
- Section heading: 22–24sp
- Body: 18–20sp
- Button: 18–20sp
- Secondary text: 16–18sp
- Metadata: 14–16sp

Typography should create a clear hierarchy without excessive decorative styling.

### Shape and Containers

- Use modest corner radii.
- Avoid excessive rounded cards.
- Avoid floating-card-heavy layouts.
- Use subtle borders where separation is required.
- Keep shadows limited and functional.
- Maintain consistent alignment and spacing.

---

## 5. Photography and Illustrations

### Prefer

- Real human/family photography
- Natural environments
- Indian context
- Older adults with family/caregivers
- Simple illustrations
- Familiar objects
- Family-provided photos
- Restrained visual cues

### Avoid

- Generic AI-generated-looking illustrations
- Futuristic medical imagery
- Overly polished artificial stock photography
- Robot/AI imagery
- Glowing brains or neural-network graphics
- Visuals that make the product look like an AI demo

The patient application should not depend on stereotypical stock imagery.

---

## 6. Motion

### Public Experience

Subtle animation is acceptable:

- Fade/slide on section reveal
- Image transitions
- Restrained hover states

### Patient Experience

Avoid:

- Pulsing controls
- Unnecessary bouncing
- Moving backgrounds
- Constant animated elements
- Decorative transitions
- Animation that competes with the user's task

Animation is appropriate when it directly supports an interaction, such as gameplay.

---

## 7. Information Density and Visual Restraint

Avoid interfaces that visually resemble AI dashboards.

Do not fill screens with:

- Excessive status chips
- Badges
- Metrics everywhere
- Decorative data visualizations
- Technical labels
- Unnecessary cards
- Dense metadata
- AI terminology used as decoration

Prefer:

- One clear primary action
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Generous whitespace
- Meaningful grouping
- Deliberate content density
- Clear labels
- Familiar interaction patterns

The interface should look intentionally designed rather than generated from a collection of UI components.

---

## 8. Anti-AI UI Rules

The following are hard visual rules:

1. No purple/blue AI gradient aesthetic.
2. No glassmorphism.
3. No glowing blobs.
4. No decorative neural-network or circuit graphics.
5. No excessive rounded cards.
6. No excessive floating UI elements.
7. No "AI-powered" repeated throughout the interface.
8. No excessive badges or pills.
9. No unnecessary emoji.
10. No mascot-heavy design.
11. No futuristic healthcare visuals.
12. No constant pulsing or decorative animation.
13. No excessive dashboards or metric-heavy layouts where they are not necessary.
14. No generic AI-generated-looking illustrations.
15. No visual element should exist merely to signal that the product uses AI.

### Final Visual Test

Before accepting a screen, ask:

> **Would this still look like a well-designed healthcare product if every reference to AI were removed?**

If the answer is no, redesign the visual treatment.
